Convert Annual Salary to Hourly Pay

Estimate your hourly equivalent and average gross pay for different pay periods. Enter your annual salary, weekly working hours and paid weeks per year. Your calculation runs in your browser.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual gross salary before deductions.
  2. Enter your usual working hours per week.
  3. Enter your paid working weeks per year.
  4. Select your currency.
  5. Press Calculate Hourly Pay to view the estimates.

Use 52 paid weeks for a typical full-year schedule. If you work seasonally or are paid for fewer weeks, adjust the number to reflect your circumstances.

Salary to Hourly Formula

Hourly equivalent = Annual salary ÷ (Weekly hours × Paid weeks per year)

Weekly average = Annual salary ÷ Paid weeks per year

Biweekly average = Annual salary ÷ 26

Monthly average = Annual salary ÷ 12

Example: $52,000 Annual Salary

Suppose you earn $52,000 annually and work 40 hours each week for 52 weeks.

  • Annual working hours: 40 × 52 = 2,080
  • Hourly equivalent: $52,000 ÷ 2,080 = $25.00
  • Weekly average: $52,000 ÷ 52 = $1,000.00
  • Biweekly average: $52,000 ÷ 26 = $2,000.00
  • Monthly average: $52,000 ÷ 12 = $4,333.33

These figures are gross estimates before taxes, insurance, retirement contributions or other deductions. Actual paychecks can vary by payroll schedule.

Why Working Hours Matter

Full-time and part-time schedules

The same annual salary can represent different hourly equivalents depending on working hours. A $52,000 salary across 2,080 annual hours equals $25 per hour. Across 1,560 annual hours, it equals about $33.33 per hour.

Paid leave and unpaid leave

Your contractual hourly equivalent may differ from a calculation based on hours physically worked. This tool uses the schedule you enter and does not interpret employment contracts or determine legal wage entitlement.

Gross salary versus take-home pay

Gross salary is pay before deductions. Take-home pay depends on applicable taxes, insurance, benefits, pension contributions and other payroll arrangements. This calculator does not estimate taxes or deductions.

Overtime and bonuses

Annual salary may exclude overtime or bonuses. When comparing job offers, check which compensation amounts are included. Overtime rules depend on your location and employment terms.

How to Compare Salary and Hourly Job Offers

Compare annual salary and hourly pay using expected working hours and paid weeks. A higher hourly rate does not necessarily mean higher annual earnings if fewer hours are available.

Example: Two Job Offers

Job A pays $52,000 annually for 40 hours per week over 52 weeks. That equals 2,080 scheduled hours and $25 per hour before deductions.

Job B pays $27 per hour for 35 hours per week over 52 paid weeks. Estimated annual gross pay is $49,140, assuming those hours are paid throughout the year.

Job A offers higher annual gross pay, while Job B has the higher hourly rate. Also compare paid leave, benefits, commuting costs and schedule flexibility.

Questions to Ask Before Accepting an Offer

  • How many hours are expected each week?
  • Are holidays and vacation paid?
  • Are bonuses or overtime included?
  • What benefits or contributions affect compensation?
  • Is the schedule consistent throughout the year?

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is $50,000 a year per hour?

At 40 hours per week for 52 weeks, $50,000 annually is approximately $24.04 per hour before deductions.

How do I convert a monthly salary to an hourly rate?

Multiply monthly salary by 12 to estimate annual salary, then divide by annual working hours. At 40 hours per week for 52 weeks, annual working hours equal 2,080.

Does this show take-home hourly pay?

No. It estimates gross hourly pay before taxes and other deductions.

Why might my employer's figure differ?

Your employer may use contractual hours, paid leave rules, payroll periods or additional compensation components that differ from your inputs.

Can I compare two job offers?

Yes, as a starting point. Also compare working hours, paid leave, benefits, bonuses and commuting expenses.

Why might my biweekly amount differ from my paycheck?

This tool divides annual salary by 26 for an average two-week period. Actual paychecks can differ because of payroll schedules, deductions or unpaid leave.
