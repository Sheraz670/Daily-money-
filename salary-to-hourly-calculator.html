Convert Annual Salary to Hourly Pay

Use this free calculator to estimate your hourly equivalent and average gross pay for different pay periods. Enter your annual salary, weekly working hours and paid weeks per year. The calculation happens in your browser.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual gross salary before deductions.
  2. Enter your usual working hours per week.
  3. Enter the number of paid working weeks per year.
  4. Choose the currency used for your salary.
  5. Press Calculate Hourly Pay to display the estimates.

For a typical full-year schedule, use 52 paid weeks. If you work seasonally or are paid for fewer weeks, adjust the number to reflect your situation. The result depends on the information you enter.

Salary to Hourly Formula

Hourly equivalent = Annual salary ÷ (Weekly hours × Paid weeks per year)

Weekly average = Annual salary ÷ Paid weeks per year

Biweekly average = Annual salary ÷ 26

Monthly average = Annual salary ÷ 12

Example: $52,000 Annual Salary

Suppose you earn $52,000 per year and work 40 hours per week for 52 weeks.

  • Annual working hours: 40 × 52 = 2,080
  • Hourly equivalent: $52,000 ÷ 2,080 = $25.00
  • Weekly average: $52,000 ÷ 52 = $1,000.00
  • Biweekly average: $52,000 ÷ 26 = $2,000.00
  • Monthly average: $52,000 ÷ 12 = $4,333.33

These figures are gross estimates before taxes, insurance, retirement contributions or other deductions. The biweekly figure is an annual average divided by 26, not a calculation based on your actual payroll calendar.

Why Working Hours Matter

Full-time and part-time schedules

The same annual salary can represent different hourly equivalents depending on working hours. A $52,000 salary spread across 2,080 annual hours equals $25 per hour. Across 1,560 annual hours, it equals about $33.33 per hour.

Paid leave and unpaid leave

Your contractual hourly equivalent may differ from a calculation based on hours physically worked. This tool uses the schedule you enter and does not interpret your employment contract or determine your legal wage entitlement.

Gross salary versus take-home pay

Gross salary is pay before deductions. Take-home pay depends on applicable taxes, insurance, benefits, pension contributions and other payroll arrangements. This calculator does not estimate taxes or deductions.

Overtime and bonuses

Annual salary may exclude overtime or bonuses. When comparing job offers, consider which compensation amounts are included in each offer. Overtime rules depend on your location and employment terms.

How to Compare Salary and Hourly Job Offers

Annual salary and hourly pay are easier to compare when you estimate the working hours and pay period for each job. A higher hourly rate does not necessarily mean higher annual earnings if the job offers fewer paid hours.

Example: Annual Salary Compared With Hourly Pay

Job A pays $52,000 annually and assumes 40 hours per week for 52 weeks. That is 2,080 scheduled hours and an hourly equivalent of $25 before deductions.

Job B pays $27 per hour for 35 hours per week over 52 paid weeks. Estimated annual gross pay is $49,140, assuming those hours are paid throughout the year and no extra compensation is included.

In this example, Job A has higher annual gross pay, while Job B has the higher hourly rate. The better choice also depends on paid leave, benefits, commuting costs, schedule flexibility and other conditions.

Questions to Ask Before Accepting an Offer

  • How many hours are expected each week?
  • Are holidays and vacation paid?
  • Are bonuses or overtime included in the advertised pay?
  • What benefits or employee contributions affect total compensation?
  • Is the schedule consistent throughout the year?

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is $50,000 a year per hour?

At 40 hours per week for 52 weeks, $50,000 per year is approximately $24.04 per hour before deductions.

How do I convert a monthly salary to an hourly rate?

Multiply the monthly salary by 12 to estimate annual salary, then divide by annual working hours. At 40 hours per week for 52 weeks, annual working hours equal 2,080.

Does this show my take-home hourly pay?

No. It estimates gross hourly pay before taxes and other deductions.

Why might my employer's figure be different?

Your employer may use contractual hours, paid leave rules, payroll periods or additional compensation components that differ from your inputs.

Can I compare two job offers?

Yes, as a starting point. Also compare working hours, paid leave, benefits, bonuses, commuting expenses and other employment conditions.

Why is the biweekly amount different from two weeks of my usual pay?

This calculator divides annual salary by 26 to show an annual average for a two-week period. Actual paychecks can differ because of payroll schedules, unpaid leave, deductions or the way an employer calculates pay.
