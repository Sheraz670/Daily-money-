What Is Body Mass Index?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a numerical measure calculated from a person's weight and height. It is commonly used as one initial screening indicator in adults, but it does not directly measure body fat or distinguish muscle from fat.

Two people with the same BMI can have different body compositions and health circumstances. BMI is best understood alongside other information, such as medical history, waist measurement, activity, and guidance from a qualified health professional.

BMI Formula

Metric: BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in meters × height in meters)

US units: BMI = 703 × weight in pounds ÷ (height in inches × height in inches)

For example, someone weighing 70 kg and standing 1.75 m tall has a BMI of about 22.9.

Common Adult BMI Categories

  • Below 18.5: Underweight range
  • 18.5 to less than 25: Healthy-weight range
  • 25 to less than 30: Overweight range
  • 30 and above: Obesity range

These are commonly used adult screening categories. They do not determine an individual's health, and interpretation may differ for some populations and clinical situations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is BMI the same as body fat percentage?

No. BMI uses height and weight only. Body fat percentage estimates the proportion of body weight that is fat and requires different measurement methods.

Is BMI suitable for children?

Children and teenagers require age- and sex-specific BMI interpretation. The adult categories on this page should not be used to assess them.

Can a muscular person have a high BMI?

Yes. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, so a muscular adult may have a higher BMI without having the same body composition as another person with that value.

Does a healthy BMI guarantee good health?

No. BMI is only one screening measure and cannot identify every health risk or replace a medical assessment.

What should I do if I am concerned about my BMI?

Discuss your result and personal circumstances with a qualified health professional, especially before making significant changes to your diet, exercise, or medical care.
