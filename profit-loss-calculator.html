This calculator compares the entered cost and selling prices. Extra expenses, fees, and taxes are not included unless you add them to the cost price.

Profit and Loss Formulas

Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price

Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price

Profit Margin = (Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Profit or Loss Percentage = (Profit or Loss ÷ Cost Price) × 100

Profit occurs when the selling price is greater than the cost price. Loss occurs when the selling price is lower than the cost price.

Example

If a product costs $80 and sells for $100, the profit is $20. The profit percentage based on cost is 25%, while the profit margin based on the selling price is 20%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between profit margin and profit percentage?

Profit margin uses the selling price as its base. Profit percentage, often called return on cost, uses the cost price as its base. They are different calculations.

What if the selling price equals the cost price?

The result is break-even: there is no profit or loss before any additional expenses.

Can the calculator handle a zero cost price?

Yes. It can show the difference between the selling price and cost, but profit or loss percentage cannot be calculated when the cost price is zero.

Does this calculate net profit?

Not automatically. To estimate profit after expenses, include relevant expenses in the cost price before calculating. Tax and accounting rules may require additional calculations.
