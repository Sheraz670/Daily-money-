How Loan Payments Are Calculated

A typical fixed-rate loan payment depends on the amount borrowed, monthly interest rate, and number of monthly payments.

Formula: Payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Here, P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal, and n is the total number of monthly payments. If the interest rate is zero, the principal is divided equally across all payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the payment include every loan cost?

No. The estimate covers principal and interest only. Origination fees, insurance, taxes, and other charges may increase the amount you pay.

What happens if the interest rate is zero?

The loan amount is divided by the total number of monthly payments, so no interest is added.

Can my actual payment be different?

Yes. Lenders may use different payment schedules, fees, compounding rules, or rounding methods. Check your loan agreement for the exact figures.

Understanding Your Loan Results

Monthly payment: The estimated amount paid each month toward principal and interest.

Number of payments: The total number of monthly instalments across the selected loan term.

Total repayment: The estimated sum of all monthly payments over the full term.

Total interest: The estimated borrowing cost calculated as total repayment minus the original loan amount.

A longer loan term usually reduces the monthly payment but can increase the total interest paid. A shorter term usually means higher monthly payments but less time for interest to accumulate.

Worked Example: A $10,000 Loan

Suppose you borrow $10,000 at a fixed annual interest rate of 6% and repay it over five years, with monthly payments and no additional fees.

  • Loan amount: $10,000
  • Annual interest rate: 6%
  • Repayment term: 5 years, or 60 monthly payments
  • Estimated monthly payment: approximately $193.33
  • Estimated total repayment: approximately $11,599.80
  • Estimated total interest: approximately $1,599.80

These figures are rounded estimates. Your lender may calculate the final payment differently because of rounding, fees, or payment dates.

What Can Affect Your Actual Loan Cost?

The calculator estimates principal and interest for a fixed-rate loan. Your actual borrowing cost may be different because of:

  • Application, origination, or administration fees.
  • Insurance, taxes, and other charges required by the lender.
  • Variable interest rates that can change over time.
  • Payment dates, rounding rules, or a final payment adjustment.
  • Late-payment charges or optional extra repayments.

Before accepting a loan, compare the annual percentage rate where available, the total amount payable, any additional charges, and the lender's repayment conditions.

Fixed-Rate and Variable-Rate Loans

A fixed-rate loan keeps the agreed interest rate unchanged for the specified fixed-rate period, which can make payments easier to plan. A variable-rate loan may become more or less expensive when its interest rate changes.

This calculator assumes a constant annual interest rate throughout the repayment term. It does not predict future rate changes or calculate lender-specific fees. For a variable-rate loan, use the result only as an illustration based on the rate you enter.

Before You Choose a Loan

  • Make sure the monthly payment fits your budget after essential expenses.
  • Compare offers using the same loan amount and repayment period.
  • Check whether the advertised rate is fixed or variable.
  • Ask about fees, early repayment rules, and late-payment charges.
  • Read the loan agreement carefully before signing.

This calculator provides general educational estimates, not financial advice or a loan offer. Confirm the exact payment and borrowing costs with your lender.
