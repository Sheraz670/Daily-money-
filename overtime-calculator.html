What Is Overtime Pay?

Overtime pay is compensation for eligible hours worked beyond a normal schedule or a threshold defined by an employment agreement or local law. Some employers pay a higher hourly rate for these hours, such as 1.5 times the regular wage.

The applicable threshold and multiplier are not the same everywhere. Check your employment contract and local labor rules before relying on an estimate for a payroll decision.

Overtime Pay Formula

Overtime hourly rate = Regular hourly wage × Overtime multiplier

Regular pay = Regular hourly wage × Regular hours

Overtime pay = Overtime hourly rate × Overtime hours

Estimated weekly gross pay = Regular pay + Overtime pay

Gross pay is the amount before taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions. This basic calculator does not determine which hours legally qualify as overtime.

Example: Overtime Earnings

Suppose your regular wage is $20 per hour, you work 40 regular hours and 5 overtime hours, and your overtime multiplier is 1.5×.

  • Regular pay: $20 × 40 = $800
  • Overtime hourly rate: $20 × 1.5 = $30
  • Overtime pay: $30 × 5 = $150
  • Total estimated weekly gross pay: $800 + $150 = $950

Your actual paycheck may differ because of taxes, deductions, bonuses, special pay rules, or different overtime eligibility requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is overtime pay calculated?

Multiply your regular hourly wage by the selected overtime multiplier, then multiply that overtime rate by the overtime hours. Add the result to your regular pay for the estimate.

What does 1.5× overtime mean?

It means the overtime rate is one and a half times your regular hourly wage. For example, a $20 regular rate becomes $30 per overtime hour.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. It estimates gross wages before tax and other deductions. Your take-home pay will usually be different.

Is overtime always paid at 1.5×?

No. The multiplier and overtime eligibility depend on the applicable law, contract, and employment circumstances. Select the multiplier relevant to your situation.

Can salaried employees receive overtime?

Eligibility varies by jurisdiction and job circumstances. Being paid a salary does not by itself answer whether someone qualifies for overtime.
