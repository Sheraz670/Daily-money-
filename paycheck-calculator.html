What Is a Paycheck Calculator?

A paycheck calculator estimates how much you may earn during a pay period. It can help you compare gross earnings with an approximate amount remaining after deductions such as taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, or other payroll deductions.

Gross pay is earnings before deductions. Take-home pay, also called net pay, is the amount left after deductions. Actual payroll calculations can be more complex than a single percentage.

Paycheck Calculation Formula

Annual gross pay for hourly workers = Hourly wage × Weekly hours × 52

Gross pay per paycheck = Annual gross pay ÷ Paychecks per year

Estimated deductions = Gross pay per paycheck × Deduction percentage

Estimated take-home pay = Gross pay per paycheck − Estimated deductions

The hourly method assumes the same hours and wage throughout 52 weeks. Unpaid leave, overtime, bonuses, and irregular hours may change actual annual earnings.

Example: Biweekly Paycheck

Suppose you earn $20 per hour, work 40 hours each week, receive 26 paychecks per year, and estimate deductions at 20%.

  • Annual gross pay: $20 × 40 × 52 = $41,600
  • Biweekly gross pay: $41,600 ÷ 26 = $1,600
  • Estimated deductions: $1,600 × 20% = $320
  • Estimated take-home pay: $1,600 − $320 = $1,280

This example is illustrative. Your actual deductions may be higher or lower.

Pay Frequency Explained

  • Weekly: usually 52 pay periods per year.
  • Biweekly: usually 26 pay periods per year, paid every two weeks.
  • Twice monthly: 24 pay periods per year, commonly paid on two set dates each month.
  • Monthly: 12 pay periods per year.

Biweekly and twice-monthly pay are different. Some months have three biweekly paydays, while twice-monthly schedules generally have two.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between gross pay and net pay?

Gross pay is the amount earned before deductions. Net pay is the amount remaining after taxes and other applicable deductions are taken out.

Does this calculator calculate exact taxes?

No. It applies the deduction percentage you enter. It does not calculate local or federal taxes, tax credits, filing status, or individual payroll contributions.

How many biweekly paychecks are there in a year?

A standard biweekly schedule has 26 pay periods in a year. Depending on the calendar and payday schedule, some years may have an additional payday for certain employees.

Can I use this calculator with an annual salary?

Yes. Select “Annual salary,” enter your yearly gross salary, choose your pay frequency, and enter an estimated deduction percentage.

Why might my actual paycheck be different?

Your actual paycheck can differ because of taxes, benefit premiums, retirement contributions, overtime, bonuses, unpaid leave, and employer payroll adjustments.
